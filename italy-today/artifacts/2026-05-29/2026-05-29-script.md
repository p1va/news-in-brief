Bank of Italy Governor Fabio Panetta delivered his "Final Considerations," the central bank's most important annual address, and his message was a sobering reality check. Panetta warned that Italy's GDP is losing momentum and that the recent Gulf War skirmishes, combined with the severe US tariffs imposed by the Trump administration last year, threaten to push Eurozone inflation past an alarming 6 percent. Crucially, Panetta warned that Europe cannot rely on Artificial Intelligence to save its economy if it does not urgently address its demographic collapse and brain drain. 

The political and media spin on this address highlights the deep anxieties currently running through the country. The financial daily Il Sole 24 Ore focuses strictly on Panetta's economic warnings, highlighting his call for the European Central Bank to recalibrate rates and his plea for Rome to avoid a destructive wage-price spiral. However, the progressive and centrist press, like La Repubblica and La Stampa, are focusing on his social alarm. They frame his speech as a dire warning to the Meloni government that closing borders and ignoring the youth exodus will hollow out the country, noting that his call for shared European debt—so-called Eurobonds—flies directly in the face of the sovereignist agenda. 

The takeaway here is that Italy is caught in a global vice. Rome is desperately trying to balance a rapidly aging, shrinking workforce against expensive global energy and a fractured geopolitical landscape. Panetta is essentially telling the political class that no amount of technological innovation or domestic political maneuvering can keep Italian factories running if there is no affordable power and no young workers to staff them.

According to investigators, the workers were recruited in India, paid barely two euros an hour, and forced to kick back portions of their wages under the threat of being deported. It is a deeply embarrassing diplomatic incident for Washington, drawing a sharp contrast between the "America First" rhetoric of the Trump administration and the reliance on exploited foreign labor to build its own sovereign outposts abroad.

Staying with the theme of labor, the government is celebrating what appears to be a historic victory, but the opposition claims it is an illusion. Prime Minister Meloni and right-wing papers like Il Giornale are taking a victory lap, claiming the data proves the right-wing coalition is successfully defeating precarious work. But look closer, and the opposition press points out the demographic catch. La Repubblica highlights that this job growth is almost entirely driven by workers over the age of 50. Meanwhile, one in three Italians remains completely inactive, a rate far higher than France or Germany. It is a tale of two economies: older Italians are staying in the workforce longer, while a forgotten generation of youth remains locked out of the system entirely.
