It was a highly symbolic flex of domestic muscle in the face of the American president's public rebukes, even as Defense Minister Guido Crosetto tried to do damage control on Radio 24, insisting that institutional relations with the US Ambassador remain solid. 

The Italian press is fiercely divided over what this rupture actually means for the country's future. On the sovereigntist right, newspapers like Il Giornale and Libero are framing Meloni's stance as a heroic defense of the homeland. They portray the Prime Minister as a resolute leader refusing to bow to American bullying, with Il Giornale praising her demand for an end to Trump's "senseless attacks." 

Conversely, the progressive and centrist opposition is sounding a massive economic alarm. La Stampa and La Repubblica warn that the emotional clash over "national pride" is blinding the country to impending retaliation. They highlight that Italy is deeply vulnerable: the US is a crucial supplier of liquefied natural gas, and Trump's aggressive 2025 global import tariffs are already strangling the European market. For the opposition, Meloni’s defiance is a dangerous geopolitical trap that risks leaving Italy diplomatically isolated and economically starved.

Moving from geopolitical threats to domestic financial realities, the Italian state is grappling with a massive internal leakage of funds. Il Sole 24 Ore reports that the Bank of Italy's Financial Intelligence Unit has flagged a staggering 32,000 suspicious operations linked directly to tax evasion and fraud over the last year. Accounting for twenty percent of all financial alerts, the bulk of these illicit movements are tied to false invoicing, rigged public tenders, and the lingering fraudulent exploitation of the Superbonus construction incentives. As the government struggles to balance the upcoming budget, the sheer scale of this shadow economy highlights the structural hurdles facing the national treasury.

Yet, when he was previously detained on Italian soil, Rome controversially released him and arranged a state flight back to Libya, effectively shielding him from The Hague. Today's sentence in Tripoli renews very uncomfortable questions about the moral and legal compromises the Italian state makes to maintain its migration agreements with North African authorities.
