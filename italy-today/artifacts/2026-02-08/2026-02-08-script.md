Finally, the culture wars have claimed another victim: comedian Andrea Pucci has withdrawn from co-hosting Sanremo following a backlash over alleged past "fascist" remarks, prompting Prime Minister Meloni to decry the "illiberal drift of the Left."

The main story today is the rapid escalation of the railway chaos from "vandalism" to "terrorism," a shift that places the Olympic Games in the crosshairs of a national security crisis.

What started yesterday as severed cables in Bologna and an arson attack in Pesaro has officially mutated into a terrorism investigation. The Bologna Prosecutor’s office, as reported by *ANSA* and *AGI*, has opened a file for "association with the purpose of terrorism and attack on transport safety." This changes the legal landscape entirely. We are no longer talking about disgruntled anarchists delaying trains; the state is now operating under the assumption of a coordinated attack on the infrastructure of the country during its highest-profile international event.

The spin war here is intense and deeply polarized. The government supporters, led by *Libero* and *Il Giornale*, are drawing a straight line between these acts of sabotage and the anti-Olympic protests that took place in Milan’s Corvetto district yesterday. They frame the protesters—who clashed with police using firecrackers—as the "moral authors" of the sabotage, accusing the left-wing opposition of being complicit by not condemning the violence harshly enough. Matteo Salvini is leading this charge, branding the dissenters as "enemies of Italy."

On the other side of the aisle, *Fanpage* and *Il Manifesto* are shouting about "criminalization of dissent." They argue that the government is using the "terrorism" label to justify a crackdown on legitimate protests against the environmental and social costs of the Games. They interview residents of Milan who feel besieged by the militarization of the city, exemplified by the presence of Qatari special forces patrolling the streets. The takeaway is that the "Olympic Truce" is dead on arrival; the Games have instead become a flashpoint for a conflict between the state’s need for order and a radicalized fringe that sees the event as a symbol of capitalist waste.

Moving from the tracks to the tribunals, the "Justice War" we covered yesterday has found a new, explosive protagonist.

Yesterday we told you about the Supreme Court (Cassazione) forcing the government to rewrite the referendum question. Today, the counter-attack from the Right has arrived, and it is personal. *La Verità* and *Il Giornale* have launched a blistering campaign against Alfredo Guardiano, a Supreme Court councilor involved in the referendum decision. They report that Guardiano is scheduled to moderate a "No" campaign event in Naples and cite leaked chats where he allegedly urged colleagues to oppose the reform. The narrative from the Right is clear: the game is rigged because the referees—the judges—are political players wearing robes.

The opposition media, particularly *La Repubblica*, defends the judiciary, framing these attacks as an intimidation tactic by a government that refuses to respect the separation of powers. However, the political reality is that Meloni has managed to secure the March date despite the hurdles. By exposing the alleged bias of specific judges, the pro-government press is trying to delegitimize the "No" camp before the campaign even begins. The message to the voter is simple: this isn't about legal technicalities; it's about breaking a "caste" that thinks it is above the law.

Meanwhile, a different kind of fracture is widening within the government coalition, specifically on the Right flank.

General Roberto Vannacci has pulled the trigger. As confirmed by *Adnkronos*, the controversial bestselling author and MEP has officially registered the statute for his independent party, "Futuro Nazionale." This is the nightmare scenario for Matteo Salvini. *La Stampa* reports panic within the League, noting that Vannacci is positioning himself to drain the "angry protest vote" that Salvini once commanded. Interestingly, *La Verità* reports a sense of calm from the Brothers of Italy camp, suggesting Meloni might view Vannacci as a useful tool to keep Salvini weak and compliant. If Vannacci runs alone and polls even 3%, he doesn't hurt the Left; he eats the League alive.

But before we go, we must touch on the "Sanremo Meltdown" that is consuming the Sunday lunch conversations.

The Festival doesn't start for a year, but the first casualty has already fallen. Comedian Andrea Pucci, tapped to co-host, has resigned after a social media firestorm accused him of sexist and "fascist" sympathies. The center-left press, like *La Repubblica*, frames this as a victory for decency. However, Prime Minister Meloni personally intervened on social media today, calling the attacks on Pucci a "frightening illiberal drift." In Italy, nothing is ever just entertainment; even a comedian quitting a song contest becomes a state affair involving the Prime Minister, proving once again that here, the culture war never sleeps.
