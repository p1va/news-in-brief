The right-wing press is spinning Ben Gvir as a rogue extremist provocateur, rather than a reflection of Israel's systemic policy. The takeaway is that Meloni's conservative government is now forced to fiercely defend left-wing, anti-establishment activists in order to protect national dignity, severely straining Rome's historical alliance with Tel Aviv.

Moving from the diplomatic tightrope to macroeconomic realities, the European Union has delivered a sobering reality check to the Italian government. The EU Commission's spring forecast has slashed Italy's GDP growth estimate for 2026 to a meager 0.5 percent, effectively making Italy the slowest-growing economy in the Eurozone due to lingering energy shocks. The progressive *La Repubblica* frames this as an absolute disaster, painting a picture of an isolated Italy suffering under stagflation. However, the conservative *Il Giornale* finds a silver lining, loudly celebrating the fact that Italy's deficit is projected to fall just below the critical 3 percent threshold. For the government, surviving the deficit rule is a political victory that staves off immediate European austerity measures, even if the broader economy is grinding to a halt.

Staying on the economic front, the future of the Italian automotive industry looks increasingly foreign. They argue that Italy is being entirely sidelined, with no new job guarantees and iconic brands left behind. Meanwhile, the business-focused *Il Sole 24 Ore* takes a clinical view, noting the necessity of the company's aggressive 6 billion euro annual cost-cutting mandate. The structural truth is that Italy's historic leverage over the former Fiat empire is effectively gone.
