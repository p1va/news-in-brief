We start with the blitz. History has been made on the ice: Arianna Fontana has become the most decorated Italian Olympian of all time, securing Gold in the mixed short track relay, a victory *Corriere della Sera* celebrates as "legendary" amidst the chaos of these Games. Speaking of chaos, the anarchist threat has materialized; following yesterday's report on the railway sabotage, a blog named "La Nemesi" has claimed responsibility for the attacks, targeting companies like Leonardo and Eni, confirming the suspicions of the Bologna prosecutors. In the media world, the presses are silent at *La Repubblica*; the paper is on strike today to protest the rumored sale of the Gedi group by John Elkann, a move the editorial committee calls a danger to democratic pluralism. On the industrial front, the news is grim for Stellantis, with *Il Fatto Quotidiano* reporting that Moody’s and S&P have cut the carmaker's rating, citing the disastrous costs of electrification, while conversely, UniCredit CEO Andrea Orcel is promising a massive 30 billion euro distribution to shareholders. Finally, the "Commando" mystery in Puglia has a twist: *AGI* reports that one of the men arrested for the military-style assault on a cash-in-transit van is a former paratrooper, suggesting that the "war on the highways" is being waged by professionals trained by the state itself.

The Lead story today is the return of a nightmare for the Meloni government: Minister of Tourism Daniela Santanchè is under investigation for bankruptcy. Again.

Just as the Prime Minister attempts to navigate the treacherous waters of judicial reform, the Milan Prosecutor’s office has opened a new front. As reported by *Corriere della Sera* and *Il Sole 24 Ore*, Santanchè is now investigated for the bankruptcy of "Bioera," a bio-food company she chaired until 2021. This is separate from, but dangerously similar to, the ongoing investigation into the collapse of "Ki Group." The accusation is that while she was a public figure, companies under her stewardship were being hollowed out, leaving debts and unpaid workers in their wake.

The reaction separates the press into two distinct realities. *Il Fatto Quotidiano* and *Fanpage* are aggressive, framing this as the definitive proof of the government's "caste" mentality. They highlight the Opposition's fury—specifically Giuseppe Conte—who argues that "those in power feel untouchable," demanding her immediate resignation. They paint a picture of a Minister who is serial in her business failures yet immovable in her seat.

On the other side of the barricade, the defense is robust and coordinated. *Libero* and *Il Giornale* frame this as yet another episode of "clockwork justice"—investigations timed perfectly to damage the Right whenever they attempt structural reforms. They quote Santanchè’s defense team calling this a "necessary technical act" and emphasize her statement: "I am calm, I continue to work." The narrative from the Right is that these are "evaluative crimes"—technical disagreements on balance sheets—being weaponized to take down a political target. However, having a Minister facing two separate bankruptcy probes simultaneously is a stress test that Meloni’s "law and order" messaging may struggle to withstand.

Moving from the courts of Milan to the halls of Brussels, the government has scored a significant, structural victory on migration.

The European Parliament has given the green light to the new regulation on "Safe Countries of Origin," a move that *Il Giornale* hails as a total vindication of the "Italian Line." The new rules allow EU nations to designate certain non-EU countries—including Egypt, Tunisia, and Bangladesh—as "safe," thereby allowing for accelerated repatriation procedures for migrants arriving from those nations. Interior Minister Matteo Piantedosi called it a "great success," noting that Europe is finally adopting the pragmatic approach Rome has championed for years.

However, the view from the Left is apocalyptic. *Libero* gleefully reports on the outrage of Laura Boldrini and the Democratic Party, who claim this vote "destroys the right to asylum." The argument from the opposition is that safety is subjective—a country safe for a straight man might be deadly for a gay man or a political dissident—and that this list is a tool to facilitate mass deportations without due process. While the humanitarian debate rages, the political reality is clear: the legal framework for the "Fortress Europe" that Meloni promised is being laid, brick by brick, with Brussels' stamp of approval.

Meanwhile, back in the Chamber of Deputies, the government is facing friendly fire from its own "loose cannon," General Roberto Vannacci.

The fallout from the formation of Vannacci’s new political group, "Futuro Nazionale," has officially reached parliament. As reported by *Adnkronos*, the government was forced to pose a "question of confidence" on the Ukraine weapons decree to avoid an ambush by Vannacci’s loyalists. The General, elected under the League’s banner but operating increasingly as an independent force, is challenging the blind support for Kyiv, positioning himself as the voice of the "pacifist" Right. *La Stampa* notes the fury of Defense Minister Guido Crosetto, calling the confidence vote a maneuver to force everyone to show their true colors. It is a sign that the coalition is fraying not from the Left, but from the Hard Right, where Vannacci is testing the waters for a future leadership challenge.

But before we go, we need to talk about vanity, Artificial Intelligence, and the President of the Republic.

In a story that feels like a modern farce, *Il Foglio* and *Il Corriere* have exposed a bizarre edit on the personal website of Marcello Minenna, the Calabrian assessor and former head of Customs. To promote his image as a statesman, Minenna published a photo of himself handing a book to President Sergio Mattarella. There was just one problem: the photo was from 2020, at the height of the pandemic, and both men were wearing masks. Minenna apparently used AI to "remove" the masks and reconstruct their faces, resulting in a grotesque image where the President looks, as per *Il Foglio*, like he has "his head stuck in his shoulders with an unreal gaze." It is a uniquely 2026 scandal: rewriting history with bad software to look better on a homepage.
