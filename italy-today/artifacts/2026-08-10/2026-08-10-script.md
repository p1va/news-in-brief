Turning from the climate to the wallet, the government has quietly pushed through a structural reform to one of Italy's most universally despised taxes: the Bollo Auto, or car tax. Buried in a new federalism decree is a total overhaul of how the tax will be paid starting in 2028. Libero and Il Messaggero report that the new system will abandon the chaotic regional deadlines and shift to a strict, 12-month rolling window starting from the exact month of a car's registration. Furthermore, the reform forces long-term rental companies to register their contracts in the public database by 2027. While it doesn't offer citizens a discount, it is a highly consequential legislative trap designed to claw back millions in lost municipal revenue from drivers who exploit regional loopholes to dodge payments.
