The Senate gave the green light to a new bill combating antisemitism, which adopts the International Holocaust Remembrance Alliance's definition. While the law passed comfortably with 105 votes, the spotlight is on the disarray within the Democratic Party. The official line from Elly Schlein was to abstain, arguing the text could limit legitimate criticism of Israeli government policy. However, as *La Repubblica* highlights, six "reformist" senators from the PD broke rank and voted in favor, aligning with the government, Italia Viva, and Azione.

The narrative gap highlights the existential crisis of the Italian Left. *Il Giornale* and the Right are seizing this as proof of the PD's ambiguity on Jewish defense, with Senators from Fratelli d’Italia accusing the opposition of being held hostage by their radical base. The passing of the bill is significant, legalizing a stricter framework for hate speech, but politically, it has exposed that the "Broad Field" alliance is still unable to find a unified voice on foreign policy and civil rights.

Moving from the Senate floor to the printing press, a historic shift is shaking the Italian media landscape.

Gedi, the publishing group controlled by the Elkann-Agnelli family, has signed a preliminary contract to sell *La Stampa*, the historic Turin daily, to the SAE Group. This is a massive symbolic moment. *La Stampa* has been the voice of the Northern industrial establishment for decades.

*La Repubblica*, which remains under Gedi for now, reports firmly on the facts, noting the sale includes local advertising networks and digital assets. However, the subtext found in *Il Foglio* and industry whispers suggests a "retreat" of the Agnelli family from their historical role as the gatekeepers of Italian public opinion. The SAE Group, led by Alberto Leonardis, has been aggregating local papers like *Il Tirreno* and *La Nuova Sardegna*.

The spin wars are subtle here. The establishment press paints this as a business reorganization to focus on *Repubblica*. But for observers of "The Machine," like those reading *Il Fatto Quotidiano*, this looks like a liquidation sale, raising questions about who will steer the editorial line of the North-West during a time of industrial decline in the automotive sector. When the Agnellis sell *La Stampa*, it’s not just a business deal; it’s a change in the country's nervous system.

But before we go, let’s leave the wars and politics for a moment of quiet wonder in Rome.

For centuries, a marble bust of Christ the Savior has sat quietly in the Basilica of Sant’Agnese fuori le mura, assumed to be the work of an anonymous craftsman. Today, researchers and the Canons Regular announced that after ten years of study, the work has been attributed to none other than Michelangelo Buonarroti. It turns out the master’s hand was hiding in plain sight on Via Nomentana all along. A reminder that in Italy, even the furniture can turn out to be a priceless masterpiece.
