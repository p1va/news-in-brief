The framing of this reform is heavily polarized. Institutional and center-right outlets present this as a necessary evolution to guarantee governability. The conservative perspective is that eliminating the run-off will prevent the endless post-election gridlocks that have historically plagued Italian politics, serving as the ultimate tool to ensure that the winner actually has the numbers to govern.

La Repubblica and Il Fatto Quotidiano frame the 42% threshold as a blatant institutional power grab by the current ruling coalition. For the opposition, removing the run-off isn't about stability; it's about allowing a minority of the electorate to seize absolute control of the Parliament.
