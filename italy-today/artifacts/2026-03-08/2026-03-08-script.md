But before we go, let's look at the actual symbol of International Women's Day in Italy: the mimosa. If you walked through any Italian city this weekend, you saw flashes of these bright yellow flowers everywhere. But the tradition isn't just a modern marketing gimmick. It was established in 1946 by three female anti-fascist partisans—Teresa Mattei, Rita Montagnana, and Teresa Noce—who were elected to help write the Italian Constitution. They chose the mimosa because it bloomed early, was resilient in harsh conditions, and most importantly, it grew wild and was free for anyone to pick in a country devastated by war. It is a humble reminder that the deepest roots of modern Italy were planted by women who fought to rebuild it.
