We start with the blitz. The Olympics are becoming a headache for the state broadcaster; the journalists of RaiSport have officially broken ranks, withdrawing their signatures from services and announcing a strike to protest Director Paolo Petrecca’s disastrous opening ceremony commentary, a mutiny *La Repubblica* describes as a "total loss of confidence" in the Meloni-appointed executive. On the slopes, the drama continues for Lindsey Vonn, who has undergone double surgery in Treviso for a femur fracture, effectively ending her comeback before it began. In geopolitics, there is a silver lining for Italy’s defense industry: *Politico* reports the Franco-German "Super Jet" project (FCAS) is dead in the water, a collapse that *Il Sole 24 Ore* notes could massively boost the rival British-Italian GCAP program led by Leonardo. Meanwhile, the domestic security alarm is ringing in Puglia after a commando unit staged a military-style assault on a cash-in-transit van near Brindisi, the fourth such attack in months, prompting *Il Fatto Quotidiano* to ask why the government is obsessed with student protesters while organized crime turns highways into war zones. Finally, the "Justice War" gets personal: FdI heavyweight Giovanni Donzelli is demanding answers after a social media account allegedly belonging to a judge mocked him as "Topo Gigio," further fueling the Right's narrative of a politicized judiciary.

The Lead story today is the confirmed escalation of the anti-Olympic sabotage campaign. What was suspected terrorism yesterday has now been claimed in writing.

A blog associated with the anarchist movement, "Sottobosko," has published a communiqué claiming responsibility for the arson attacks on the railway network that paralyzed Bologna over the weekend. The message is chillingly direct: "Fire to the Olympics and to those who produce them." As reported by *Corriere della Sera*, the text attempts to justify the leap from street protests to infrastructure sabotage by arguing that "peaceful dissent is no longer effective." This claim validates the hypothesis of the Bologna prosecutors, who had already opened a file for terrorism, but it also fundamentally shifts the security posture of the Games. We are now dealing with an adversary that views the Olympic infrastructure—cables, trains, power lines—as legitimate military targets.

The reaction in the press is a study in polarization. The Right is claiming total vindication. *Il Giornale* and *Libero* are running victory laps, essentially saying "We told you so." They link this specific act of sabotage to the broader "social centers" and student movements, conflating the violent anarchists with the protesters who clashed with police in Milan’s Corvetto district. Matteo Salvini has seized the moment to demand not just prison time, but financial restitution for every minute of delay caused to commuters. The narrative here is simple: dissent against the "Nation's Event" is treason.

Conversely, the Left is in a defensive crouch. *La Stampa* and *Il Manifesto* are careful to condemn the violence but warn against a "blanket criminalization." Their fear, articulated by several commentators, is that the government will use these arson attacks as a pretext to crush legitimate, non-violent protest against the environmental impact of the Games. They highlight that the "Sottobosko" blog represents a tiny, radicalized fringe, unconnected to the thousands of residents worried about housing costs and cementification. However, with "Fire to the Olympics" now in the public domain, the space for nuanced debate on the Games' impact has likely vanished.

Moving from the enemies of the state to the rot inside the state, a massive scandal is brewing at the Ministry of Economy.

In a story that *Il Sole 24 Ore* and *La Repubblica* are treating with grave seriousness, Stefano Di Stefano, a high-ranking Director at the Ministry of Economy (MEF) and a board member of Monte dei Paschi di Siena (MPS), is under investigation for insider trading. The accusation is stunning in its brazenness: prosecutors allege that while representing the state—which is the main shareholder of the bank—he purchased shares in MPS and Mediobanca just before a sensitive public offer was launched. If true, this isn't just financial crime; it is a betrayal of the institution.

The political fallout is dampened for now, but potentially explosive. *Il Giornale* is noticeably quiet on the political responsibility, focusing on the individual act, while the opposition press is beginning to ask how Minister Giancarlo Giorgetti’s department could harbor such a conflict of interest. The investigation revealed these trades after seizing Di Stefano’s phone for a separate probe, suggesting this might be the tip of an iceberg. For foreign investors watching Italy, seeing a Treasury official betting on the very bank he overlooks is a disastrous signal of governance failure.

From white-collar crime to the new frontier of labor exploitation, the Milan prosecutor has placed the delivery giant Glovo under "judicial administration."

This is a landmark decision reported by *La Stampa* and *Il Fatto Quotidiano*. The accusation is "caporalato"—illegal gangmastering—but digital style. The investigation found that Glovo was effectively treating 40,000 riders as slaves to an algorithm, paying them an average of 2.50 euros per delivery. The prosecutors argue that the sheer volume of control the app exerts over the riders—tracking times, punishing delays, managing shifts—makes them de facto employees, not freelancers. By placing the company under administration, the state is effectively saying the company’s business model is illegal under the Italian constitution. This is a direct shot across the bow of the entire Gig Economy, signaling that the "technological veil" can no longer hide medieval labor practices.

But before we go, we have to check in on the never-ending soap opera of the former Minister of Culture, Gennaro Sangiuliano. Just when you thought it was safe to ignore the gossip columns, the courts have dragged it back to the front page. Maria Rosaria Boccia, the influencer who brought down the Minister, has been indicted for stalking and aggravated injuries. Sangiuliano, playing the role of the martyr, told *Il Messaggero* that he has suffered "a year of unspeakable violence." It is a uniquely Italian trajectory: a political scandal that morphs into a criminal trial, ensuring that the embarrassing details of the government's private life will continue to be aired in public courtrooms for years to come.
