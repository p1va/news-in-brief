The family requested a second opinion from the prestigious Bambino Gesù hospital in Rome. Today, the family lawyer announced on national television that Rome has deemed the child "no longer transplantable," effectively stating there is no hope.

However, the Monaldi hospital in Naples is fighting back. In a dramatic split, the local medical team insists the child is still viable for a new heart and is keeping him at the top of the national emergency list. *Il Mattino* and local sources portray a desperate defense of the southern hospital's capabilities, while the national press paints a picture of a family caught in the crossfire of medical liability and conflicting diagnoses. It is no longer just a story of malpractice; it is a harrowing standoff over who gets to decide when hope is officially over.

Turning to a story bubbling under the surface of the mainstream but exploding in the right-wing press, a new front has opened in the government's war on illegal immigration.

Now, the "enemy" identified by the Right is the medical establishment. *La Verità* calls this "sabotage by white coats," framing it as an ideological boycott by public servants against state laws. The Left and medical associations have yet to mount a full coordinated defense, but expect this to become the next flashpoint: the criminalization of medical discretion when it intersects with border control.
