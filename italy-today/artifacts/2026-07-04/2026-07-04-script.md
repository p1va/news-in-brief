The media framing of this historic visit presents a textbook divide in Italian politics. The progressive press, led by La Repubblica and Il Fatto Quotidiano, is treating the Pope's words as a direct, moral indictment of the right-wing government's stringent border policies and its ongoing crackdown on NGO rescue ships. They highlight the Pope's phrase that migrants are "victims of disinterest and corruption." On the other side, conservative outlets like Il Giornale are deliberately softening the political edge of the visit. Instead of framing it as a rebuke, they focus heavily on institutional harmony, prominently noting that Undersecretary Alfredo Mantovano officially welcomed the pontiff on behalf of the Italian government, painting the event as a shared moment of national compassion rather than a geopolitical lecture.

The ultimate takeaway is that the Vatican is actively reasserting its moral authority on the frontline of the migration crisis. By physically returning to Lampedusa, the Church is putting gentle but unignorable pressure on European capitals, reminding them that while they debate quotas and border walls, the Mediterranean remains a graveyard.
